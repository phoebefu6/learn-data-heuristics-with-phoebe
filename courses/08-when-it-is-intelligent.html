When the product is intelligent

Tideline's AI panel says one thing: "Enterprise churn is being driven by seat under-use." Nothing else. Not what the model can do, not how well it does it, not why it said this, not how to correct it. Those four gaps are D11 through D14, and Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction is the set built to catch exactly this shape of failure. Tonight we teach the 18, learn Apple's four-dichotomy triage for deciding which of them matter most on a given feature, look honestly at what Google PAIR does and does not add on top, and rebuild the panel live.

Part 0

Where we are

Seven layers of the review instrument are built. Session 2 named the decision a screen exists to support. Session 3 gave the data a contract, an owner and lineage. Sessions 4 and 5 checked the platform and the operating promise underneath it. Sessions 6 and 7 covered the surface a reader actually looks at. Every one of those lenses can inspect Tideline's charts, numbers and panels. None of them can inspect a claim a model makes about the data, because none of them were written for a system that talks back. That is the layer tonight adds, and it is the last content layer before session 9 assembles everything into one instrument.

★ What you walk out with tonight. Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction, grouped by the four phases of an interaction, and which four of them a bare AI panel almost always fails first. Apple's four dichotomies as a triage step that tells you which of the 18 to fight for. And an honest look at what Google PAIR adds on top of Microsoft here, which is not more catches.

LiveTideline's AI panel, in full3 min▶

Open Tideline's overview screen and there is a small panel titled "AI insight". It renders one sentence: "Enterprise churn is being driven by seat under-use." There is no second sentence. No badge, no footnote, no button. That is the entire feature, and it carries four distinct defects at once:

  • D11 - it never says what the panel can do. Is it forecasting, explaining, or ranking causes? A reader cannot tell the difference between a lucky guess and a modelled result.
  • D12 - it never says how well it does it. No accuracy figure, no sample size, no time window the claim was measured over.
  • D13 - no explanation of why it said this particular thing about this particular account or period.
  • D14 - no way to correct or dismiss the claim if a reader knows it is wrong.
Real world

A customer-success lead read that sentence in a renewal call and told the account it was "an AI finding", as if that settled the matter. The account's actual churn driver, confirmed two weeks later, was a failed SSO migration. Nothing on the panel would have told her to doubt the sentence, because nothing on the panel says how often it is right.

Self-studyWhy this layer is last, not first3 min▶

The human-AI guidelines look, at first glance, like they belong next to session 6 and 7's interaction and surface lenses - they are also about what a reader sees on screen. The reason they get their own session is that a claim is a different kind of object than a chart. A truncated axis misleads about a number that already exists. An unqualified AI sentence misleads about a number the system is asserting on its own authority, with no ground truth the reader can check by eye. That difference is why a dedicated set of guidelines exists for it at all, and why session 9's instrument treats these four checks as their own row rather than folding them into the interaction lens.

Part 1 · covers Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction

Eighteen guidelines, four phases of one interaction

7 min live

Microsoft's set is the centrepiece of tonight because of how it was built, not just what it says. Amershi, Weld, Vorvoreanu, Fourney, Nushi, Collisson, Suh, Iqbal, Bennett, Inkpen, Teevan, Kikin-Gil and Horvitz distilled it from more than 150 individual design recommendations scattered across industry and research, then validated the surviving 18 with 49 practitioners reviewing 20 popular AI products. It won an Honorable Mention at CHI 2019. That provenance, a large pool reduced and then tested against real products, is exactly the discipline session 1 taught for Nielsen's ten, and it is why this set outranks a vendor blog post promising "12 tips for trustworthy AI".

The honest arithmetic. Session 1 measured that switching on Microsoft's 18 lens takes Tideline's catch count from 11 to 14, a gain of three. There are four defects owned by this session, D11 through D14, so it looks like the lens should add four. It does not, because D14, no way to correct or dismiss the claim, is also a violation of NN/g's third heuristic, user control and freedom, which the usability lens already caught back in session 1. Microsoft's 18 genuinely finds D11, D12 and D13 for the first time. It shares D14 with a set switched on earlier. That is not a weaker result, it is what an honest instrument looks like: overlap gets named, not hidden.

Self-studyWhy 49 practitioners across 20 products matters3 min▶

Plenty of "principles for trustworthy AI" documents exist that are one person's opinion formatted as a numbered list. Microsoft's process was closer to Nielsen's: gather a large pool of candidate recommendations, more than 150 of them pulled from research papers, internal design reviews and public guidelines, then reduce to the set that a panel of practitioners could actually apply, and check that the reduction still held up against real shipped products rather than hypothetical ones. Twenty products is a small number of validations by industry standards, but it is twenty more than most competing lists ever ran, and the paper says exactly which twenty.

Part 2 · covers what a claim must carry

Four things a claim must carry, or it is not a claim

7 min live

Four of Microsoft's 18 map directly onto Tideline's four defects, and together they define the minimum a system is allowed to assert before a reader should act on it: what it can do, how well it does that specific thing, why it said this particular sentence, and how to push back if it is wrong.

LiveThe four, mapped onto D11 through D144 min▶
GuidelineThe question it answersMissing on Tideline as
G1 - capabilityWhat kind of thing is this: a forecast, an explanation, a ranking?D11
G2 - accuracyHow often is this right, measured against what, over what period?D12
G11 - reasonWhy this account, why this period, why this specific driver?D13
G9 - correctionHow does a reader who knows better tell the system so?D14

Notice the order. Capability and accuracy come before the claim is even trusted. The reason comes with the claim itself. Correction comes after, when the claim turns out to be wrong. A panel that only ever adds one of the four, usually the reason, still leaves a reader unable to judge whether to believe it in the first place.

Self-studyWhy "how well" needs a measurement basis, not a percentage3 min▶

A bare "94% accurate" is nearly as unhelpful as no number at all, because it does not say accurate at what, against what ground truth, or over what window. G2 is satisfied by a sentence like "right about the top driver in 71 of the last 100 flagged accounts, checked against confirmed cancellation reasons over the last two quarters", because that sentence tells a reader exactly how much weight the claim can bear and exactly what would make it stale. The same discipline applies to G11's because-clause: "seat count fell more than 30 percent in 60 days while login weeks stayed flat" is a reason a reader can check against the account. "Our model flagged this" is not a reason, it is a restatement of the claim.

Part 3 · covers Apple's triage and the PAIR overlap

A triage step, and a guidebook that adds nothing new here

7 min live

Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for machine learning is not a numbered list of principles, and treating it as one would misstate the source. It is a classification framework: four dichotomies you place any ML feature on before you design it. The value is triage, not coverage. Sorting a feature onto these four axes tells you which of Microsoft's 18 guidelines to fight hardest for, because a small complementary feature nobody notices does not need the same rigour as a feature steering a renewal call.

LiveReading the four axes on a real feature4 min▶

Tideline's churn panel lands toward critical, proactive and visible: it is shown without being asked for, it can change what a renewal conversation looks like, and it sits in the middle of the main screen. That placement is what makes G2 and G11 non-negotiable rather than nice to have, because a wrong, unexplained claim on a critical proactive feature costs more than the same claim tucked into a settings page. A quieter feature, a background job that silently flags anomalous usage spikes for the data team, lands toward complementary, reactive and invisible. It only has to satisfy G14, adapt cautiously, because almost nobody sees it act.

Real world

A team once spent a design sprint adding a full explanation panel, a confidence score and a feedback form to an invisible background deduplication job nobody had ever complained about, while the customer-facing churn panel next door still had none of the three. The dichotomies exist so that effort like that goes where the placement actually demands it.

Self-studyWhat Google PAIR adds, and what it does not3 min▶

Google PAIR's People + AI Guidebook is six chapters: user needs and defining success, data collection and evaluation, mental models, explainability and trust, feedback and control, errors and graceful failure. It is a real, primary-verified, well-regarded guide. Measured on Tideline, it adds precisely nothing on top of Microsoft's 18: the catch count stays at 14. Everything PAIR's chapters can flag here, mostly explainability and feedback concerns, was already flagged by Microsoft's guidelines.

That is not PAIR being weak, any more than IBCS was weak in session 1. Two well-built sets aimed at the same layer will overlap, and PAIR's real contribution is upstream of any single guideline: its "user needs and defining success" and "mental models" chapters are about deciding what to build before you get to guideline-level detail like G1 through G18. On a product that already has an AI feature shipped, that upstream work has already happened, which is exactly why the overlap shows up as zero new catches rather than PAIR being irrelevant.

Build-along

Rebuild Tideline's AI panel

★ 16 min · everyone builds

One sentence becomes four honest statements. Work through the steps in order, writing each one down before you look at the next, then place the finished feature on Apple's four axes.

Write the capability sentence, G1. One line that says what kind of thing the panel does, in plain language: a forecast, a ranked list of likely causes, a detector. State what it does not do just as clearly - Tideline's rebuilt sentence explicitly says it is "not a recommendation".

Write the accuracy statement, G2. Give a number and its measurement basis together: right how often, checked against what ground truth, over what window. A percentage with no basis fails this step even if it looks precise.

Write the because-clause, G11. Name the specific signal that fired for this specific account or period, in terms a reader can go check themselves against the account's own numbers.

Add the correction control, G9. A visible way to say "not the driver" or dismiss the claim outright, and a one-line promise about where that feedback goes.

Place the feature on Apple's four dichotomies. Critical or complementary, private or public, proactive or reactive, visible or invisible. Write down where Tideline's panel lands on each of the four.

Say which guidelines that placement makes non-negotiable. A critical, proactive, visible placement should point you straight back at G2 and G11 as the two you would refuse to ship without, exactly as it does for Tideline.

Add the four AI rows to the review instrument. Capability stated, accuracy stated with basis, reason attached to the specific claim, correction control present. Session 9 runs these four rows over Tideline alongside everything sessions 2 through 7 built.

Resist the urge to write the accuracy statement last. Teams that write the confident-sounding sentence first and the honesty number second almost always soften the number to fit the sentence. Writing G2 right after G1, before the because-clause exists to distract you, keeps the number closer to true.

Homework

Before session 9

20 min
  • Find one AI or model-driven claim in a product you use or ship. Check it against all four: does it say what it can do, how well, why this claim, and how to correct it?
  • For any guideline it fails, place the feature on Apple's four dichotomies first, then decide whether that failure is urgent or merely untidy. A complementary, invisible feature failing G11 is a different problem than a critical, proactive one failing it.
  • Write the because-clause for that claim yourself, in one sentence, using only signals a reader could check. If you cannot write one that holds up, that is the finding to bring to session 9.
  • Bring your four AI rows. Session 9 runs the complete nine-session instrument over Tideline, and your own product's rows sit well next to it.
Check yourself

Three questions

What is the actual difference between guideline G1 and guideline G2 in Microsoft's 18?

G1 is a capability claim: what kind of thing this is. G2 is a performance claim attached to that capability: how often it is right, and against what. Tideline's panel had neither, which is why it registers as two separate defects, D11 and D12, not one.

Adding Google PAIR's six chapters on top of Microsoft's 18 changes Tideline's catch count from 14 to what?

It stays at 14. PAIR is a real, well-built guide, but on a feature that already shipped, its distinctive contribution, defining success and mental models, sits upstream of any individual finding. Overlap between two good sets aimed at the same layer is the norm, exactly as IBCS added nothing on top of Tufte and Few in session 1.

What job do Apple's four dichotomies do that Microsoft's 18 guidelines do not?

Apple's set is a classification framework, not a numbered guideline list, so it is never cited as "N principles". Placing a feature as critical, proactive and visible, as Tideline's churn panel is, is what tells you G2 and G11 are non-negotiable rather than merely nice to have.
